# Arabic Core

Use this reference for language mechanics and sentence-level correctness.

## Orthography

Check:
- همزة القطع والوصل in standard spelling;
- `أ / إ / آ / ا` where the word requires it;
- `ة` versus `ه`;
- `ى` versus `ي`;
- accidental elongation `ـ`;
- duplicated letters caused by typing;
- hidden direction or zero-width characters when they create visible defects.

Do not add full diacritics unless requested. Preserve necessary diacritics in names, quotations, religious text, or terminology when present.

## Agreement

Check:
- subject–verb agreement;
- noun–adjective gender and number;
- pronoun antecedents;
- demonstratives and relative pronouns;
- dual/plural consistency;
- number–noun constructions when the wording depends on them.

Do not force artificial case endings into undiacritized prose.

## Syntax

Repair:
- missing predicates or subjects;
- ambiguous pronoun references;
- broken coordination;
- excessive coordination with `و` when it obscures hierarchy;
- literal translation syntax;
- overloaded sentences containing several independent logical moves.

Split sentences at logical boundaries, not at arbitrary word counts.

## Punctuation

Prefer Arabic punctuation in Arabic prose:
- comma `،`;
- semicolon `؛`;
- question mark `؟`;
- colon `:`;
- full stop `.`.

Do not replace punctuation inside URLs, code, formulas, identifiers, citations, or official English names.

Avoid:
- spaces before punctuation;
- missing space after punctuation when prose continues;
- a comma between subject and predicate without a structural reason;
- repeated punctuation unless the tone intentionally requires it.

## Numbers

Preserve the user's numeral style unless asked to normalize it.
Do not silently convert dates, currencies, percentages, or decimal conventions.
Keep units attached to the correct values.
Do not infer missing units.

## Names and terminology

Do not "correct" proper nouns by intuition.
Preserve official names, standards, product names, model names, regulations, and acronyms.
Choose one translation for recurring technical terms and use it consistently.
