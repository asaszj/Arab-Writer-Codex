# Naturalness

Natural Arabic is not the absence of formal phrases. It is writing in which wording, structure, and rhythm serve the meaning rather than a template.

## Detect weak formulaic patterns

Review:
- generic scene-setting before the actual point;
- repeated thesis statements with synonym changes only;
- identical paragraph lengths and transition patterns;
- forced symmetry such as `من جهة... ومن جهة أخرى...` when ideas are not true counterparts;
- three-item lists created only for rhetorical balance;
- stacked abstract nouns;
- generic conclusions that add no synthesis;
- excessive headings for very short sections;
- bolding most nouns;
- repeated `label: explanation` bullets.

## Replace abstraction with action

Weak:
`العمل على تعزيز وتطوير وتحسين مستوى...`

Prefer:
`نطوّر...` / `نحسّن...` / `نرفع...`

Use an abstract noun when the concept itself is the subject, not merely to make prose sound formal.

## Transitions

Use explicit logic:
- cause;
- contrast;
- consequence;
- chronology;
- condition;
- exception;
- priority.

Do not insert a transition at every paragraph opening. Sometimes adjacency is enough.

## Rhythm

Vary sentence length according to complexity.
Keep a short sentence when emphasis benefits from it.
Keep a longer sentence when clauses belong together and remain easy to parse.

## Conclusions

A useful conclusion should do at least one of:
- synthesize;
- decide;
- recommend;
- state implication;
- identify next action;
- identify remaining uncertainty.

Delete conclusions that only repeat the introduction.

## Humanization requests

If a user says "humanize" or "remove AI style":
- improve naturalness and voice;
- reduce repetition and generic framing;
- do not claim detector avoidance;
- do not intentionally introduce errors, slang, or inconsistency to imitate a human.
