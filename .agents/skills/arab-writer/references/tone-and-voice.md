# Tone and Voice

Treat tone as dimensions rather than canned personas.

## Infer these dimensions

Estimate from the user's request and source:
- formality: casual ↔ formal;
- directness: soft ↔ direct;
- warmth: reserved ↔ warm;
- brevity: compact ↔ expansive;
- authority: tentative ↔ decisive;
- technical density: general ↔ specialist;
- emotional intensity: neutral ↔ expressive.

Do not maximize all dimensions.

## Voice lock

When preserving voice:
1. Note typical sentence length.
2. Note recurring vocabulary and level of formality.
3. Note first/third person use.
4. Note whether the author prefers declarative, explanatory, or conversational rhythm.
5. Note punctuation habits that are not errors.
6. Keep those traits while repairing defects.

Do not preserve accidental typos, broken grammar, or factual inconsistencies as "voice."

## Tone change

When the user requests a new tone, change only the dimensions needed.
Examples:
- "أكثر مهنية" does not mean colder or more bureaucratic.
- "أكثر ودية" does not mean verbose.
- "أقوى" does not mean more certain than the evidence.
- "مختصر" does not mean abrupt if courtesy is required.

## Sensitive communication

For complaints, refusals, escalation, or conflict:
- state the issue concretely;
- separate facts from interpretation;
- avoid unnecessary accusation;
- keep requested remedy explicit;
- preserve dates, orders, ticket numbers, and commitments.
