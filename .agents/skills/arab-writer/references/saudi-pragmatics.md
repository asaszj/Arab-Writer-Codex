# Saudi Pragmatics and Register

Use when the intended audience, source voice, or requested locale is Saudi. The objective is pragmatic fit, not decorative localization.

## First classify the register
Choose the closest class from evidence in the source/request:
- `ksa-institutional-msa`: government, boards, official letters, policies, formal nonprofit/corporate material;
- `ksa-professional-conversational`: WhatsApp/Teams/Slack/business messages that are professional but not bureaucratic;
- `ksa-dialectal`: everyday or explicitly dialectal writing;
- `arabic-neutral`: pan-Arab audience where Saudi localization is unnecessary.

For dialectal content, preserve the source region only when it is known or evidenced. Do not infer Najdi/Hijazi/Southern/Northern/Eastern identity from one ambiguous token.

## Pragmatic checks
Before finalizing, verify:
1. **speech act** — request, refusal, complaint, invitation, thanks, escalation, recommendation, or decision remains the same;
2. **hierarchy** — wording matches peer/subordinate/senior/external recipient relationship when known;
3. **request strength** — `نأمل`, `نرجو`, direct imperative, or colloquial request is not strengthened/weakened accidentally;
4. **courtesy** — formal courtesy is proportional and not padded with ceremonial language;
5. **regional scope** — do not insert a localism into neutral MSA or flatten requested dialect into MSA;
6. **ambiguity** — avoid culturally plausible but unsupported interpretations; if a phrase can mean more than one thing, preserve or neutralize rather than invent.

## Common failure modes
- over-formalizing an ordinary Saudi business message;
- translating colloquial particles literally into stiff MSA;
- inserting stereotyped slang to appear local;
- confusing professional directness with rudeness;
- adding honorifics/titles not supplied by the user;
- changing `نأمل الرفع` into a generic `يرجى اتخاذ اللازم` when the organizational action matters;
- flattening pragmatic nuance while keeping literal meaning.

## Evidence standard
Saudi quality claims require native/human review or a Saudi benchmark. Lexical familiarity alone is not sufficient evidence of pragmatic competence.
